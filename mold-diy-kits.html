Mold DIY kits: not clearance, and when to hire

There is no federal mold license and no EPA numeric “safe” mold count for homes. Moisture control is the real control. A consumer kit answers a narrow question about one sample. It does not map hidden growth, find the water source, write a remediation plan, or certify that cleanup is finished.

Sources: EPA Brief Guide to Mold, Moisture and Your Home; EPA Mold Cleanup in Your Home.

What a kit actually measures

Most consumer mail-in kits collect a tape-lift, swab, or bulk piece (sometimes a settling dish). The lab reports what it sees on that sample. Mold spores are common outdoors and indoors. “Present” is often unsurprising. “Not detected on this tape” is not a whole-house all-clear.

Post-remediation clearance is a separate inspection after work — usually by an assessor who did not do the cleanup. A kit you run yourself is not that inspection. In New York, Labor Law Article 32 requires an independent licensed assessment to define scope and forbids the same licensee from assessing and remediating the same project.

EPA’s homeowner size rule of thumb: if the moldy area is less than about 10 square feet (roughly 3 ft × 3 ft), most people can clean hard surfaces themselves after fixing the water problem. Larger areas, HVAC contamination, sewage/flood water, or sensitive occupants are reasons to hire.

When a kit can be a first look

  • You see a small spot, you can dry the source, and you want a lab to name what is on that surface.
  • You are not in a real-estate, insurance, or landlord dispute that demands a licensed report.
  • You understand one sample is not clearance and not an air survey of the whole building.

When DIY is wrong — hire a licensed assessor

  • Area larger than about 10 sq ft, mold in HVAC, or water from sewage/flood.
  • Buying, selling, or documenting a rental and someone will ask for a licensed report.
  • You need a written remediation plan or post-remediation assessment.
  • Infants, elderly, pregnant people, or people with asthma / allergies / weakened immunity are in the household and you want professional judgment.
  • A kit result is confusing, positive everywhere you sample, or collected from the wrong surface.

Only Florida, Texas, New York, and Louisiana run a state mold license that splits (or separately licenses) assessment and remediation. Elsewhere, a private trade-group logo is not a state license.
