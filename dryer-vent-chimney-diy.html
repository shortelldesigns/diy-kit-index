Dryer vent · chimney

DIY dryer-vent cleaning vs hire — and chimney inspection limits

Lint in a dryer exhaust duct is a fire risk. Creosote and damaged flues are a different system. This page separates what a flexible-rod kit can reach from when you should hire, and what a homeowner should not pretend a chimney “inspection” is. Not fire-code or insurance advice.

Sources: NFPA Clothes Dryer Safety tip sheet; CSIA Certified Dryer Exhaust Technician; CSIA chimney guidance via CSIA.

Dryer vents: lint kit vs hire

NFPA’s consumer guidance is to clean lint out of the vent pipe about once a year — or sooner if clothes take longer than usual to dry — and to keep the area around the dryer clear. Failure to clean is a leading factor in dryer-involved home fires in NFPA research summaries.

Situation DIY flexible-rod kit Hire (e.g. CSIA CDET)
Short, accessible duct you can see end-to-end Often enough for routine lint removal Optional; still useful if you want a camera check
Long run, crushed duct, roof/soffit termination you cannot see May leave lint beyond the rod reach Preferred — technician can inspect termination and airflow
Recurring overheating, burning smell, or slow dry times after cleaning Stop guessing Hire — may be duct design, not just lint

CSIA Certified Dryer Exhaust Technician® (CDET®) is a separate credential from CSIA Certified Chimney Sweep® (CCS®). A person may hold one, both, or neither. Verify on CSIA’s public finder. A clean lint screen does not mean the hidden duct is clear.

Chimneys: inspection limits for DIY

NFPA 211 defines levels of chimney inspection (typically described as Level I, II, III) that trained chimney professionals use. A homeowner looking up the flue with a flashlight is not a Level II camera inspection after a weather event, chimney fire, or before lighting an unused appliance. Do not “DIY sweep” with improvised weights if you cannot control what falls into the appliance or living space.

When to hire a credentialed sweep (and verify the card): annual check before heating season; after any chimney fire; before using a fireplace or stove that sat idle; after storms that may have shifted the chimney; when you smell smoke indoors or see damaged mortar/caps.
