Power outage · winter

Winter power-outage kit: battery power, light and a weather radio

By Shortell Designs · Updated October 2026

A winter outage can disrupt communications, water and transportation, cause food spoilage, and prevent use of medical devices. Ready.gov advises taking an inventory of what you need that runs on electricity, planning batteries and alternate power such as a portable charger or power bank, and having flashlights for every household member. (Ready.gov)

Compare the five outage-kit picks

Safety first: heat, generators and carbon monoxide

  • Use a generator ONLY outdoors. Generators and fuel should be at least 20 feet from windows, doors and attached garages; keep the generator dry, connect appliances with heavy-duty extension cords, and let it cool before refueling. (Ready.gov)
  • Never use portable generators inside homes or garages, even if doors and windows are open; exhaust should face away from the home. (CPSC)
  • Do not use a gas stove or oven to heat your home; it increases the risk of carbon monoxide poisoning. Generators, camp stoves and charcoal grills belong outdoors, at least 20 feet from windows. (Ready.gov)
  • Install CO alarms with battery backup on every level of the home (Ready.gov), and after a snowstorm keep dryer, furnace, stove and fireplace vents clear of snow. (NFPA) Compare options on our carbon monoxide alarms page.
  • Disconnect appliances and electronics; power may return with surges. Go to a community location with power if the cold is extreme, and plan ahead for refrigerated medicines and power-dependent medical devices. (Ready.gov)
