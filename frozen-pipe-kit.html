Frozen pipes · winter

Frozen-pipe kit: pipe sleeves, heat cable, faucet covers and a freeze alert

By Shortell Designs · Updated November 2026

Compare the five frozen-pipe picks

Water expands as it freezes, and the American Red Cross lists simple steps that cost little: let cold water drip from a faucet served by exposed pipes, because a trickle helps keep them from freezing; keep garage doors closed when water lines run through the garage; keep the thermostat at the same temperature day and night; and if you will be away in cold weather, leave the heat on at no lower than 55°F. The Red Cross says the higher heating bill is far cheaper than a burst-pipe repair. The kit below covers the rest: foam pipe sleeves for exposed runs, UL-listed heat cable where insulation is not enough, outdoor faucet covers and a freeze alert.

Water expands as it freezes, and that pressure can break metal or plastic pipes. The pipes that freeze most often are outdoor hose bibs and sprinkler lines, supply pipes in unheated basements, crawl spaces, attics, garages and kitchen cabinets, and pipes on exterior walls with little insulation. (American Red Cross) Ready.gov's winter checklist includes learning how to keep pipes from freezing. (Ready.gov)

Safety first: thawing, burst pipes and heat cable

  • Never thaw a pipe with an open flame. Do not use a blowtorch, kerosene or propane heater, charcoal stove or other open-flame device. Safer heat: an electric heating pad wrapped around the pipe, a hair dryer, a portable space heater kept away from flammable materials, or towels soaked in hot water. Keep the faucet open while you thaw. (American Red Cross) (Maine MEMA)
  • If a pipe bursts, shut off the water and call a plumber. Know how to shut off the water in case pipes burst; stopping the flow can minimize damage. Call a plumber and contact your insurance agent. (Maine MEMA) If you suspect frozen pipes, turn off the water at the main shut-off valve; if you turn on a faucet and nothing comes out, call a plumber. (Michigan MI Ready)
  • Can't find or reach the frozen section? Call a licensed plumber. (American Red Cross)
  • Watch for shock near water. Do not use electrical appliances in areas of standing water. (Michigan MI Ready) (Maine MEMA)
  • Heat cable: listed products, used as intended. Use heat tape or thermostatically controlled heat cable approved by an independent testing organization such as Underwriters Laboratories, only for its intended use (exterior or interior), and follow the maker's installation instructions. (Michigan MI Ready) The Red Cross also suggests pipe sleeves or UL-listed heat tape or heat cable on exposed pipes. (American Red Cross)

Thawing with a portable space heater? CPSC says to keep it at least three feet from drapes, furniture and bedding and to plug it straight into a wall outlet. (CPSC) More in our space heater safety checklist.

Free steps before you buy anything

  • Remove, drain and store outdoor hoses; close the inside valve to each hose bib and leave the outside bib open to drain. (American Red Cross)
  • In very cold weather, let cold water drip from a faucet served by exposed pipes, open kitchen and bathroom cabinet doors (move cleaners out of children's reach), and keep garage doors closed if supply lines run there. (American Red Cross)
  • Keep the thermostat at the same setting day and night; if you travel, leave the heat on no lower than 55°F. (American Red Cross) (Maine MEMA)
  • Seal leaks that let cold air reach pipes, such as gaps around wiring, dryer vents and pipes. (Michigan MI Ready)
  • Away for a while? Have someone check daily that the heat is on, or shut off and drain the water system. Michigan notes that shutting off water also deactivates a home fire-sprinkler system. (Maine MEMA) (Michigan MI Ready)
