Wildlife · exclusion, not capture

Wildlife exclusion — after the animals are gone

Exclusion means closing the entry so wildlife cannot return. It is the right DIY step after the cavity is empty. Sealing first can trap animals in insulation, force them to chew a new hole into living space, or leave dependent young behind. This page does not teach illegal trapping or relocation.

Confirm vacancy before you seal

  • No sounds for several nights.
  • No fresh droppings at the entry.
  • When bats, raccoons, or other species that may have young are involved — and you cannot rule out a nursery — hire a licensed operator instead of guessing.

Do not trap illegally. In many states, live-trapping wildlife (especially to relocate it) requires a commercial nuisance-wildlife / problem-animal license. “Catch and dump” raccoons down the road is often illegal and spreads disease risk. This page will not give a live-trap how-to, a relocation route, or a poison recipe.

State law, briefly

Rules vary by state. Paid wildlife control is a licensed occupation in many places (examples from Shortell’s wildlife index: Ohio CNWACO, New York NWCO, Massachusetts PAC). A hunting or trapping license is not the same as a commercial wildlife-control credential. Read your state wildlife agency rules before you act on your own property.

When to hire

  • Animals (or young) may still be inside.
  • Bats in living space, or any rabies-vector species you cannot handle safely under local law.
  • You need commercial methods that require a license in your state.
  • Repeated re-entry after DIY patches — entry points you have not found.

For transcribed license lists in selected states, see Shortell’s Wildlife Control Index (separate site). Always verify the current license with the issuing agency.
